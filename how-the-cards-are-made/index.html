How the cards are made

Every look in the Lookbook is one card with six parts. Here is one card, Contre-Jour, taken apart.

01
PART 01 / NAME

Name

A look gets the name working directors and photographers already use for it. When a technique has no common name, it gets a plain one that says what you see. Never a living artist's name.

CONTRE-JOUR · NAME
Contre-Jour
02
PART 02 / THE WORDS

The words

The words you paste: short, concrete, written so an image or video model renders the look the same way every time. Vague words that every model ignores are not allowed.

CONTRE-JOUR · THE WORDS
contre-jour, shooting straight into the sun, subject in near-silhouette with a glowing halo edge, lens flare, haze
03
PART 03 / THE EXAMPLE

The example

One frame made from those exact words with an image model, or a frame from our own footage, so you can see the look before you use it. Weak examples are re-made or dropped, and every one is logged.

CONTRE-JOUR · THE EXAMPLE
a cyclist silhouetted against a blazing low sun, rim light on the wheels, lens flare
04
PART 04 / WHAT IT IS NOT

What it is not

The line that separates this look from the one next to it, so you pick the right card instead of the nearly right one.

CONTRE-JOUR · WHAT IT IS NOT
Not underexposure: the light source sits in or near the frame.
05
PART 05 / MOODS AND PAIRS

Moods and pairs

Moods come from a short fixed list, so the deck can deal a look from a feeling. Pairs are the cards that sit well beside it.

CONTRE-JOUR · MOODS AND PAIRS
dreamy · melancholy  /  pairs with shoegaze, expired film, Art Nouveau display

PART 06 / THE CHECKS

The checks

Before a card ships it passes the build: the words stay within a length limit, banned words are caught, every page and link must resolve, and the same card is tested through the connector that AI assistants use.

CONTRE-JOUR · THE CHECKS
length · banned words · names · links · connector test
