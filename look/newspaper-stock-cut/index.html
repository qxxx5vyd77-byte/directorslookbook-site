Newspaper Stock Cut

Also called: 19th century newspaper cut, stock cut vignette, classified ad engraving, printer's cut, old newspaper advertisement illustration

What it is

A small stock illustration of the kind 19th-century newspapers kept in a drawer and dropped into advertisements: a ship, a locomotive, a pointing hand. Heavy black engraved lines with coarse parallel hatching, a single word in fat-face capitals above it, ink squash on yellowed newsprint, nothing else on the page.

The prompt

Paste it into an image or video prompt as is, then add your own subject.

Prompt

19th century newspaper stock cut, small black engraved vignette of one object with coarse parallel hatching, heavy ink squash, one word in Victorian fat face capitals above it, yellowed newsprint with faint show-through, no colour, plain and sturdy

Not this

What it gets confused with, or how it fails. Use it as a negative prompt where the tool takes one.

Not this

Not fine copperplate and not an art woodcut: the lines are coarse and workmanlike, the object is generic, and the composition is a stamp-sized vignette, not a scene. No colour, no gradients, no halftone dots. One word only; it stays a cut, not a poster.
